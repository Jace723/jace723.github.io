STQP

Segmentation, Targeting, Question Mapping, Positioning

What is STQP?

DefinitionSTQP is a strategic model, proposed by Jace Shim in Winner Takes the Answer (2026), consisting of four steps, Segmentation, Targeting, Question Mapping, and Positioning, that designs positioning by connecting a brand’s strengths and evidence to the decision questions and applicable conditions of target customers.

Proposed byJace Shim (심준규)

What Question Mapping is, and is not

Question Mapping is not generating piles of questions with AI and turning them into blog titles. It is the strategic judgment of linking customer, decision, conditions, claims, and evidence. STQP does not replace STP: the S and T steps stay the same. The list of evidence produced at P connects VRIO-D, which chooses the resources, with E-E-A-T-C, which turns that evidence into writing that holds up when read in pieces.

How STQP relates to existing theory

What it inherits

STP (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning), the marketing flow systematized by Philip Kotler.

What it adds

Question Mapping (Q), inserted between Targeting and Positioning as an independent step with its own deliverable, the question map. Question Mapping lays out what target customers ask, for which decisions, under what conditions, and connects those questions to the company’s strengths and evidence.

Application case

Chapter 6 of Winner Takes the Answer looks at Ramp, the corporate spend management company. Finance staff at small and large companies use the same words, “spend management,” yet ask different things: ease of setup without dedicated staff, versus complex approval chains and accounting-system integration. Ramp split questions by customer size and answered each separately. The chapter provides a template for drawing a question map.

Propositions

These propositions are theoretical claims to be tested through real cases and research, not laws already verified.

  1. Even when the target customer is the same, if decision questions and applicable conditions differ, the evidence needed for positioning may differ.
  2. When the relationship between positioning and questions is clear, resources can be concentrated on the evidence needed rather than on content that repeats the same claims.
  3. The more the conditions a product supports match the conditions its positioning promises, the more the gap between customer expectations and actual experience can be reduced.

Scope and limitations

Especially useful

Markets where different decision structures are mixed within the target customer; fields where customers ask AI long questions.

Less useful, or use with caution

Caution: splitting questions too finely blurs the brand promise. What differs from question to question is the angle of evidence and explanation, not the promise of who the company is.

Claims STQP does not make

STQP does not claim that choosing good questions guarantees citation, or that content costs fall by a fixed percentage.

Frequently asked questions

What is STQP?

What does the Q in STQP mean?

Q stands for Question Mapping: laying out what target customers ask, for which decisions, under what conditions, and connecting those questions to the company’s strengths and evidence. Its deliverable is a question map.

How is STQP different from Kotler’s STP?

STQP keeps Segmentation and Targeting unchanged and inserts Question Mapping before Positioning. Positioning in STQP centers on the customer questions a company can answer most persuasively and leaves behind a list of evidence for every claim.
