3C-AI

Company, Customer, Competitor + AI as information intermediary

What is 3C-AI?

Definition3C-AI is a strategic analysis model, proposed by Jace Shim in Winner Takes the Answer (2026), that examines the relationships among company (Company), customer (Customer), and competitor (Competitor) while also reviewing how AI’s selection of information and composition of answers affect the strengths and comparison set conveyed to customers. It treats AI not as a fourth competitor but as an intermediary that selects and relays information.

Proposed byJace Shim (심준규)
SourceWinner Takes the Answer (2026), Chapter 4
TypeStrategic analysis model
The question it asksOnce AI steps in, who becomes our competitor?

Components of 3C-AI

ElementTraditional 3C asks3C-AI adds
CompanyWhat are our strengths, weaknesses, resources, and capabilities?Are the strengths we know we have also visible in public information? AI reads only what has been published.
CustomerWhich segments exist, by age, occupation, income, or company size, and what does each need?Which constraints are hidden in the questions customers actually ask?
CompetitorWhich companies sit in the same industry and product category?Which alternatives does the customer’s question bring into the comparison? Questions, not industry classifications, draw the boundaries of competition.
AI (intermediary)Not part of the original map.How does AI’s selection and composition of answers shape the comparison set and the strengths conveyed to customers?

Two competitions

Viewed through 3C-AI, competition appears in two layers: competing on products and competing on explanations. The first is competition between actual products and services. The second is competition between the explanations conveyed to customers on the basis of published information. The two are connected but do not necessarily coincide.

How 3C-AI relates to existing theory

What it inherits

3C analysis, the strategic analysis framework set out by Kenichi Ohmae (Company, Customer, Competitor).

What it adds

AI’s role as an information intermediary. 3C-AI does not add AI as a fourth C. It treats AI as the party that sets up the field of competition: it decides which companies are compared together, which advantages are put up front, and which conditions are left out.

Application case

Chapter 4 of Winner Takes the Answer analyzes Zapier, the workflow automation company. Zapier created an introduction page for each combination of connected apps, matching the questions customers actually asked, and later built comparison content around them. The chapter also shows how a reservation software firm discovered that, for a solo business owner’s question, AI may recommend a free shared calendar first: change the question and the competitor changes.

Propositions

These propositions are theoretical claims to be tested through real cases and research, not laws already verified.

  1. As the constraints in customer questions change, the comparison set and supporting explanations a company must strategically review may also change.
  2. When the conditions under which published strengths apply are unclear, the gap between actual competitiveness and the comparative explanation conveyed to customers may widen.

Scope and limitations

Especially useful

Markets where customers ask AI to compare alternatives before contacting a company.

Less useful, or use with caution

Markets where customers and companies are connected through long relationships, or where the offline experience is decisive. Check how much your industry’s customers actually ask AI before deciding how intensively to apply it.

Claims 3C-AI does not make

3C-AI does not claim that AI answers always widen the scope of competition. It is not a tool for inferring how an AI system works internally; it reads the shape of the comparison that appears in the answer.

Frequently asked questions

What is 3C-AI?

3C-AI is a strategic analysis model, proposed by Jace Shim in Winner Takes the Answer (2026), that examines the relationships among company (Company), customer (Customer), and competitor (Competitor) while also reviewing how AI’s selection of information and composition of answers affect the strengths and comparison set conveyed to customers. It treats AI not as a fourth competitor but as an intermediary that selects and relays information.

Is AI a fourth competitor in 3C-AI?

No. 3C-AI treats AI as an intermediary that selects and relays information among company, customer, and competitor, not as a fourth C. In most markets AI does not sell products in a company’s place; it decides which products to show customers and on what criteria to compare them.

How is 3C-AI different from Kenichi Ohmae’s 3C analysis?

3C-AI keeps Ohmae’s Company, Customer, and Competitor, and adds one question to each so that AI’s role as an information intermediary is drawn onto the map. It redraws the competitive map from customer questions rather than from an industry classification.
