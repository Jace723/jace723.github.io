E-E-A-T-C

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness, Citability

What is E-E-A-T-C?

DefinitionE-E-A-T-C is a content model, proposed by Jace Shim in Winner Takes the Answer (2026), that adds Citability to Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness, designing trustworthy information so that its meaning, conditions, and source are preserved even when it is lifted out and used in generative answers.

Proposed byJace Shim (심준규)
SourceWinner Takes the Answer (2026), Chapter 7
TypeContent model
The question it asksWhen our explanation gets summarized, does our true picture survive?

Components of E-E-A-T-C

PerspectiveWhat it asksUnit of judgmentSource
Experience (E)Has the writer experienced it firsthand?Content and writerGoogle
Expertise (E)Does the writer have the knowledge and skill of the field?Content and writerGoogle
Authoritativeness (A)Is it recognized as a trustworthy source on the topic?Writer and siteGoogle
Trustworthiness (T)Is it accurate, honest, and safe?The content as a wholeGoogle
Citability (C)Are meaning, conditions, and source preserved when lifted out and used?Paragraphs and sentencesExtension proposed in Winner Takes the Answer

The three conditions of Citability

ConditionWhat it requires
Independent understandingA paragraph lifted out and read on its own still makes clear what it is about and who is claiming what. Avoid openings such as “This method” or “The feature mentioned above.”
Preservation of conditionsThe conditions under which a claim holds, such as country, period, product version, pricing plan, target customer, and test environment, sit right next to the claim.
Traceability of sourcesReaders can find the original data behind a claim, how it was measured, who is responsible for it, and when it was confirmed. The mere presence of a link is not enough.

E-E-A-T-C is not an official selection criterion adopted by Google or any other AI service. E-E-A-T is Google’s perspective; the C is an extension proposed in Winner Takes the Answer for reviewing companies’ content. C does not replace the four perspectives: making untrustworthy content easy to cite only spreads wrong information more effectively.

How E-E-A-T-C relates to existing theory

What it inherits

E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness), the perspective Google has described for judging content quality.

What it adds

Citability (C): the property by which information keeps its meaning, conditions, and source when it is lifted out and used as an explanation in answer to a question. Citability is not a count of how many times something has been cited. Its goal is content that is hard to cite wrongly, not content that is cited a lot.

Application case

Chapter 7 of Winner Takes the Answer analyzes how Stripe, the online payments company, explains easily confused concepts, such as payment processor, payment gateway, and merchant account, by drawing clear distinctions. The chapter opens with a case in which a “95%” figure travelled into AI answers without its conditions.

Propositions

These propositions are theoretical claims to be tested through real cases and research, not laws already verified.

  1. Even trustworthy material may be insufficiently prepared to be used accurately in generative answers if independent understanding, preservation of conditions, and traceability of sources are difficult.
  2. Stating conditions and evidence close to the claim can make it easier for customers and reviewers to check the scope of application of an explanation.
  3. Only by combining a format that is easy to cite with sufficient evidence of trustworthiness can the intended value and actual conditions of delivery be conveyed consistently.

Scope and limitations

Especially useful

Reviewing pages, reports, and documentation whose paragraphs are likely to be quoted in AI answers, especially where prices, eligibility, or test conditions matter.

Less useful, or use with caution

What a company controls is the citability of its original content; the citation accuracy of actual AI answers is a separate matter. Some explanations need to be long, and compressing complex judgments into one sentence loses necessary relationships.

Claims E-E-A-T-C does not make

E-E-A-T-C does not claim that a particular format guarantees citation, or that shorter is always better.

Frequently asked questions

What is E-E-A-T-C?

E-E-A-T-C is a content model, proposed by Jace Shim in Winner Takes the Answer (2026), that adds Citability to Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness, designing trustworthy information so that its meaning, conditions, and source are preserved even when it is lifted out and used in generative answers.

What does the C in E-E-A-T-C stand for?

C stands for Citability: the property by which information keeps its meaning, conditions, and source when it is lifted out and used as an explanation in answer to a question. It consists of three conditions: independent understanding, preservation of conditions, and traceability of sources.

Is E-E-A-T-C a Google ranking factor?

No. E-E-A-T is Google’s perspective for judging content quality. E-E-A-T-C, with Citability added, is an extension proposed by Jace Shim in Winner Takes the Answer for reviewing companies’ content. It is not an official criterion of Google or any AI service.
