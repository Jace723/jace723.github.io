VRIO-D

Value, Rarity, Inimitability, Organization, Discoverability

What is VRIO-D?

DefinitionVRIO-D is a strategic model, proposed by Jace Shim in Winner Takes the Answer (2026), that adds Discoverability to the Value, Rarity, Inimitability, and Organization of a company’s resources, analyzing whether the value and distinctiveness those resources create can be discovered and verified in AI-mediated information search. Discoverability consists of three layers: access, understanding, and verification.

Proposed byJace Shim (심준규)
SourceWinner Takes the Answer (2026), Chapter 5
TypeStrategic model
The question it asksAre our strengths visible to customers?

Components of VRIO-D

QuestionWhat it asksDirection of view
V · ValueDoes the resource produce useful results for customers?Inside → outside
R · RarityDo competitors readily have it too?Inside ↔ competitors
I · InimitabilityIs it difficult and costly to copy?Inside ↔ competitors
O · OrganizationIs there a system for using the resource?Inside
D · DiscoverabilityCan customers discover and verify that value in public information?Looking in from outside

The three layers of Discoverability

LayerWhat it asksWhat the problem looks like
AccessIs the information published where crawlers can read it and people can find it?Information exists only in images, PDFs, or behind logins.
UnderstandingCan customers understand the value of the resource from the published information?Only dimension tables and model names, with no explanation of what they mean to the customer.
VerificationIs evidence such as test conditions, certifications, or customer cases provided alongside the claim?Only claims like “top quality,” with no conditions or results.

The three layers stack in sequence. Without access there is no chance to understand; without understanding there is no reason to verify. Diagnosis is therefore most efficient from the bottom layer up. D is distinct from O: O usually assesses how resources are used inside the company, while D reveals the gap between that internal use and the external explanation customers can verify.

How VRIO-D relates to existing theory

What it inherits

VRIO, the framework set out by Jay Barney within the resource-based view of the firm (Value, Rarity, Inimitability, Organization).

What it adds

Discoverability (D) and its three layers: access, understanding, and verification. D evaluates not the resource itself but the public explanation of the value that resource creates. It is not a demand to disclose everything: critical processes stay protected while their results and scope of application become verifiable.

Application case

Chapter 5 of Winner Takes the Answer examines how Salesforce, the customer relationship management (CRM) software company, has built its “State of” research report series into a knowledge asset, following how data inside a company turns into trust outside it. The chapter opens with a parts-company example whose resources passed all four VRIO questions yet remained invisible in public information.

Propositions

These propositions are theoretical claims to be tested through real cases and research, not laws already verified.

  1. In AI-mediated purchase searches, when public evidence to verify the value and distinctiveness of resources is lacking, a gap may open between actual competitiveness and customer understanding.
  2. The capability to connect disclosable results to customer questions can help existing resources reach purchase decisions.

Scope and limitations

Especially useful

Markets where customers compare alternatives using public information; companies seeking new and overseas customers, such as small and mid-sized exporters.

Less useful, or use with caution

Markets where long trading relationships and hands-on experience are decisive. A high D cannot compensate for weak V and R.

Claims VRIO-D does not make

VRIO-D does not claim that D is a necessary condition for every competitive advantage, or that being discovered guarantees a sale. It is not a formula for calculating search performance.

Frequently asked questions

What is VRIO-D?

What does the D in VRIO-D stand for?

D stands for Discoverability: whether customers can discover and verify, in public information, the value and distinctiveness a resource creates. It has three layers: access, understanding, and verification.

How is VRIO-D different from Barney’s VRIO?

VRIO’s four questions start inside the company and look outward or at competitors. VRIO-D adds a fifth question that looks into the company from the outside, from where customers and AI stand. A low D does not erase V, R, I, or O, but it can keep a company off the first candidate list.
