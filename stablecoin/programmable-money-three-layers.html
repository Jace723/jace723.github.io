What makes a stablecoin payment programmable?

Definition · Value–Purpose–Evidence ModelThe Value–Purpose–Evidence Model, proposed by Jace Shim (2026) in the working paper Programmable Money in Three Layers, identifies three responsibilities in conditional settlement: the value layer provides the means of settlement, the purpose layer defines the condition the parties agreed to fund, and the evidence layer determines whether that condition has been met. A stablecoin payment becomes programmable when a specified rule can govern the release of funds based on evidence that a system can evaluate.

Principal hypothesisModularity is most valuable when evidence requirements change frequently while the underlying settlement obligation remains stable. Integration can have lower costs when a program has few exceptions and its evidence sources rarely change.

Unit of analysisAn individual conditional claim and the institutions responsible for resolving it

A deposit is easy to count. A completed service is harder.

A donor, public agency, or business may need to commit funds before the event that justifies payment. A stable-value token can make funds available for settlement, but its transfer record provides limited information about what happened outside the payment system.

Respite care

A municipal agency funds an hour of respite care. A missing attendance entry might reflect a failed terminal, a late submission, or a service that never happened. A system that treats every missing entry as nonperformance will produce a clean ledger and may leave legitimate invoices unpaid.

Software milestone

A payment system can settle an invoice immediately after authorization, while the buyer and developer may disagree about whether the submitted software meets the milestone.

Both examples raise the same design question: which part of a payment system should change when parties revise what counts as evidence?

The three layers

LayerWhat it decidesWhat belongs here
Value (V)The unit, amount, funding, and redemption routeSettlement asset such as a fiat-referenced stablecoin; custody, liquidity, settlement finality, and conversion costs
Purpose (R)What the parties agreed to fundPayer and permitted payee, amount or budget, the event that triggers payment, and authority to grant exceptions or resolve disputes
Evidence (E)Whether the agreed event happened, in a form the decision process can useClaims with a source, an observation time, and a status: sufficient, incomplete, or disputed

A signed claim establishes who made a statement and whether it changed in transit; whether the claim accurately describes the service depends on the evidence process and its oversight. An implementation can use one ledger or several systems. Functional separation means that the settlement asset, rule, and evidence source can be identified and changed under documented procedures, and authorization is treated as a distinct decision even if one institution operates multiple layers.

When does separation pay off?

Compare two ways to process the same contracts: an integrated system in which the payment provider controls the rule and verification process, and a modular system with documented boundaries between those functions.

ACost of adapting to changes in contracts or evidence sources
HExpected harm from mistaken payment decisions
KCost of coordinating and operating the system

Modular is better when (reduction in A) + (reduction in H) > (additional K)

Report the components separately, because some design choices shift costs from the payer to the provider.

The modular advantage depends on usable interfaces, a versioned rulebook, and sufficient review capacity.

Three testable propositions

P1

Evidence changes

Holding the settlement rail fixed, programs that change evidence sources more often should see larger reductions in reconfiguration time from a modular design. Measure staff hours, interruption time, and the number of affected integrations.

P2

Review and error

Moving incomplete claims to a time-limited review queue should reduce mistaken denials relative to immediate rejection. Compare the reduction with delays, administrative cost, and possible improper releases, and independently adjudicate a sample of claims to estimate error in each direction.

P3

Settlement asset

Holding the policy and evidence process constant, stablecoin settlement should improve end-to-end performance only where gains such as more reliable timing or lower transfer costs exceed custody, conversion, reconciliation, and exception costs. Compare against conventional transfers using the same business rules.

Pilot design

Assign comparable contracts to different combinations of rule architecture and settlement rail. Record time from service completion to usable funds, authorization errors, rule-update costs, and review outcomes. Where a randomized comparison is impractical, compare phased changes with similar contracts processed under the original arrangements, and track merchants who decline participation as well as those who complete a transaction.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a stablecoin payment programmable?

According to the Value–Purpose–Evidence Model proposed by Jace Shim (2026), a stablecoin payment becomes programmable when a specified rule can govern the release of funds based on evidence that a system can evaluate. The token provides the means of settlement, the payment agreement defines the condition, and a separate evidence process determines whether the condition has been met.

What are the three layers of programmable money?

The value layer covers the unit, amount, funding, and redemption route, including custody, liquidity, settlement finality, and conversion costs. The purpose layer defines what the parties agreed to fund: payer, permitted payee, budget, triggering event, and authority over exceptions and disputes. The evidence layer turns events into claims with a source, an observation time, and a status of sufficient, incomplete, or disputed.

When should programmable payment systems be modular rather than integrated?

The paper’s principal hypothesis is that modularity is most valuable when evidence requirements change frequently while the underlying settlement obligation remains stable. A modular design improves measured performance if its reductions in adaptation cost (A) and expected harm from mistaken payment decisions (H) together exceed any additional coordination and operating cost (K). Integration can cost less when a program has few exceptions and stable evidence sources.

Why can’t a stablecoin transfer record prove that a service was delivered?

A transfer record shows when and how much was paid, but provides limited information about what happened outside the payment system. A missing attendance entry might reflect a failed terminal, a late submission, or a service that never happened, so treating every missing entry as nonperformance can produce a clean ledger while leaving legitimate invoices unpaid.

Does a digital signature verify that a claim is true?

No. A signed claim establishes who made a statement and whether it changed in transit. Whether the claim accurately describes the service depends on the evidence process and its oversight.
