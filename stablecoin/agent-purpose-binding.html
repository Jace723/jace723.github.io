What is agent–purpose binding?

Definition · Agent–Purpose Binding ModelThe Agent–Purpose Binding Model, proposed by Jace Shim (2026), is a conditional payment arrangement in which the funder specifies the service to be purchased, the provider submits a claim against that purpose, and a designated reviewer can resolve conflicting evidence before the disputed amount is released. The agreement gives the recipient or an authorized representative a practical way to report an error. Here, “agent” refers to the service provider in a principal–agent relationship.

Core ideaStablecoins can carry the approved transfer, while the decision to release funds remains with the parties specified in the agreement.

Each party knows something the others cannot immediately verify.

Provider

Holmström’s (1979) work on observability and incentives frames what information is worth using when a principal cannot observe an agent’s actions directly. The care setting adds a further concern: the beneficiary can have direct knowledge of the visit and still lack practical means to challenge a decision.

Releasing the supported portion

MThe agreed ceiling for an installment
AThe amount already supported by sufficient evidence, with 0 ≤ A ≤ M

Release A now · Reserve up to M − A for adjudication

Where the visit is indivisible, A is zero until the visit is approved or the reviewer authorizes a correction. If several visits appear on one invoice, verified visits can be released while the others are reviewed. A separate authorization check precedes settlement, so a decision can be amended before funds are sent.

When does a stablecoin help?

Can help

A fiat-referenced stablecoin can settle an approved claim where the payer and provider can access a reliable payment and redemption route. The practical gain depends on the time between approval and funds becoming usable; for a small provider that includes receiving funds, converting them if necessary, and reconciling the transfer against an invoice.

Can add cost

Where bank transfers already settle promptly and cheaply, introducing a token could add steps and costs.

What travels with the payment

In a controlled record system

  • Care evidence, with an assisted alternative for beneficiaries who cannot use a digital interface
  • Visit times, location, images, and health details stay here

With the payout

  • A transaction reference
  • The amount

Publishing care details alongside the payment would create needless exposure, and the proposal can be evaluated without biometric data. A signed attendance record establishes provenance, while accuracy still depends on how the observation was made and how corrections are handled.

Outcomes include the share of supported claims paid within the service standard, the share of unsupported payments, provider days awaiting usable funds, time to resolve appeals, and recipient burden. Separate time spent verifying care from time spent settling an approved claim. The denominator for rejection rates should include claims providers abandoned after discovering a documentation burden, and outcomes should be compared by provider size and access to assisted channels.

Predictions

1

Partial release

Partial release should reduce the days of delay for verified portions of multi-visit invoices. Its value should be greatest when disputes are concentrated in a minority of visits.

2

Assisted review

An assisted review path should recover more valid claims in settings with intermittent documentation or limited device access, with review costs tracked alongside recovered payments.

3

Failure test

If these effects do not appear, or provider participation falls enough to reduce access to care, the approval design has failed the proposed test.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Agent–Purpose Binding Model?

The Agent–Purpose Binding Model, proposed by Jace Shim (2026), is a conditional payment arrangement in which the funder specifies the service to be purchased, the provider submits a claim against that purpose, and a designated reviewer can resolve conflicting evidence before the disputed amount is released. The agreement gives the recipient or an authorized representative a practical way to report an error. Here, “agent” refers to the service provider in a principal–agent relationship.

Who should authorize payment for home care?

In the Agent–Purpose Binding Model, the parties named in the payment agreement decide. The funder sets the service, installment amount, and acceptable evidence; the provider claims against that purpose; a designated reviewer, independent of the provider’s financial interest in the disputed amount, resolves conflicting evidence; and the recipient or a representative can report an error. A stablecoin can carry the approved transfer, but it does not make the release decision.

What happens when a care visit record is incomplete?

Incomplete records lead to time-limited review rather than immediate payment or immediate rejection. A missed check-out can be documented through an assisted channel, and conflicting accounts move to review with reasons recorded and a route to challenge the decision.

How does partial release work?

For an installment with an agreed ceiling M, let A be the amount already supported by sufficient evidence. The rule releases A and reserves up to M − A for adjudication, where the program permits partial settlement. In the paper’s example, four visits billed at 40 units each have three supported records, so 120 units can be paid while a reviewer checks the remaining 40.

When does a stablecoin help in care payments?

The practical gain depends on the time between approval and funds becoming usable, which for a small provider includes receiving, converting, and reconciling the transfer. Where bank transfers already settle promptly and cheaply, introducing a token could add steps and costs.

Should care visit data be recorded on a blockchain with the payment?

No. The care evidence belongs in a controlled record system with an assisted alternative for beneficiaries who cannot use a digital interface. The payout needs only a transaction reference and amount; publishing visit times, location, images, or health details alongside the payment would create needless exposure. The proposal can be evaluated without biometric data.

How does this model relate to Electronic Visit Verification?

Electronic Visit Verification, which applies to certain in-home Medicaid services in the United States, is a modest precedent for collecting factual visit records. A visit record can help establish attendance but may leave questions about the scope of work or a disputed change in the care plan, which the model addresses through decision rights and review.
