When should an AI agent settle each call?

Definition · Payment-Frequency Break-Even RuleThe payment-frequency break-even rule, proposed by Jace Shim (2026) for usage-based payments made by AI agents, separates four decisions (authorization, metering, billing frequency, and settlement) and compares settling each API call, settling batches of metered calls, and buying a subscription. With the same fixed settlement cost on both paths, batching is cheaper when (N − B) × f exceeds L, the expected incremental cost of batch collection delay, nonpayment, and reconciliation.

The short answerPer-call settlement is most plausible when use is sporadic or sellers lack a reliable way to collect later. At high volumes, a small fixed fee on every payment can outweigh the cost of carrying a capped unpaid balance.

Google’s Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) illustrates a payment-agnostic approach to recording delegated authority, and the x402 white paper describes an HTTP-based approach to requesting and presenting payment for internet resources. These protocols motivate the application; the comparison asks what each billing arrangement costs when the same service, authority, and usage records are held fixed.

Rules for delegated spending

RuleWhat it specifies
Delegated budgetA buyer can delegate purchases of a specified API up to a budget, set a maximum unit price, and define when the permission expires
Unique referenceEach service request carries a unique reference
Billable eventsA contract or service agreement defines whether a completed result, a timed-out request, or a retry is billable; when the result is uncertain, both records can be reconciled before charging again
Budget accountingThe budget covers settled and pending charges, with a provision for known fees
RefundsA delayed refund should not restore spendable authority until the accounting records confirm it

Per-call settlement fits

Sporadic purchases from new vendors, where access requires immediate settlement and the cost per transfer is small, or where sellers lack a reliable way to collect later.

Batching fits

Recurring high-volume relationships when collection risk is modest. Where a vendor will not extend credit, a small prepaid balance shifts the exposure to the buyer, who can limit the amount held and specify how unused funds are returned.

Frequently asked questions

When should AI agents pay per API call?

According to the model proposed by Jace Shim (2026), per-call settlement is most plausible when use is sporadic or sellers lack a reliable way to collect later, for example a one-off lookup from a new vendor that requires immediate settlement where the cost per transfer is small. Recurring high-volume relationships are predicted to favor aggregation when collection risk is modest.

What is the payment-frequency break-even rule?

The payment-frequency break-even rule, proposed by Jace Shim (2026) for usage-based payments made by AI agents, separates four decisions (authorization, metering, billing frequency, and settlement) and compares settling each API call, settling batches of metered calls, and buying a subscription. With the same fixed settlement cost on both paths, batching is cheaper when (N − B) × f exceeds L, the expected incremental cost of batch collection delay, nonpayment, and reconciliation.

What are the four decisions in a usage-based payment?

Authorization answers who may buy, from whom, for what purpose, and within what budget. Metering determines which events count as billable. Billing frequency determines when charges are grouped. Settlement determines how the vendor receives funds. A system can meter every call while transferring stablecoins only when a recipient-specific amount or time limit is reached.

How much can batching API payments save?

In the paper’s illustrative example, 1,000 calls at 0.02 units each carry a usage charge of 20. With a transfer cost of 0.004, per-call settlement adds 4 in fees, while ten batch settlements cost 0.04. Batching can absorb up to 3.96 units of incremental delay, collection, and reconciliation cost before it loses its fee advantage.

What if an API vendor will not extend credit to an AI agent?

A small prepaid balance is an alternative. It shifts the exposure to the buyer, who can limit the amount held and specify how unused funds are returned.

How should an agent’s spending authority handle retries and refunds?

Each request carries a unique reference, and the service agreement defines whether a completed result, a timed-out request, or a retry is billable. When the result is uncertain, records are reconciled before charging again. The budget counts settled and pending charges, and a delayed refund does not restore spendable authority until the accounting records confirm it.
