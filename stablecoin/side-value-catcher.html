What is Side-Value Catcher tokenomics?

Definition · Side-Value Catcher ModelSide-Value Catcher tokenomics (사이드-밸류 캐처 토크노믹스), proposed by Jace Shim in Target Money (2026), is a dual-token design in which a payment token is kept as a stable, regulation-compliant settlement instrument, while a separate governance token captures value from the side of the system: platform fee revenue and interest on reserve assets. The two layers are kept apart so that governance-token volatility cannot impair payment stability.

Why separateA single asset cannot be both a stable medium of exchange and an instrument whose value rises with the ecosystem. The book separates the two into distinct capital layers.

Two separated capital layers

Payment token (KRWC)

Settles care, childcare, freelance, and voucher payments at a fixed value. It pays no yield to holders, and its reserves are held in safe assets.

Governance token (GOVC)

Holds governance rights and captures value generated alongside the payment network. It is not collateral supporting the payment token’s peg.

The Terra–Luna lessonThe book argues that the 2022 Terra–Luna collapse showed what happens when a stablecoin’s stability depends on the market value of a sister token: redemptions trigger new issuance, prices fall, and the two assets collapse together.

The book also argues that, as payment stablecoin issuance concentrates among banks and large technology firms, a separate governance layer is how other innovators can retain a stake in the rules and uses of the network.

How the side value is captured

CF_total = Σ ( V_domain,k × R_fee,k ) + ( A_reserve × I_treasury )

TermMeaning in the book
V_domain,kQuarterly payment volume in each domain k: elder care, childcare, education, vouchers, freelance services
R_fee,kThe platform fee rate charged in that domain (0.5% on average in the book’s design)
A_reserveAverage reserve assets held one-to-one for the payment token
I_treasuryInterest earned on short-dated government and monetary-stabilization instruments

ΔS = − CF_total / P

In the book’s design, the quarterly cash flow buys back governance tokens and burns them, so the change in supply equals the cash flow divided by the market price. The book calls the resulting path, in which the number of tokens retired per period shrinks as the price rises, the asymptotic deflation model.

Governance safeguards

SafeguardDesign in the book
Fixed supplyTotal governance supply fixed at genesis, with no further minting
Checkpointing vote-weightVoting power based on holdings recorded before a proposal, combined with holding period through a logarithmic function, to resist flash-loan and Sybil attacks
Qualified on-chain validatorsRewards limited to holders who perform verifiable work, such as merchant due diligence or parameter verification

Risk backstop

If the collateral ratio of the payment token falls below 99%, the book’s design pauses ordinary governance withdrawals and triggers emergency settlement. Reserved governance tokens are pledged to pre-approved market makers and over-the-counter institutions rather than sold on open markets, in exchange for fiat liquidity that goes to the redemption vault.

As a second line, the design accumulates a fiat surplus buffer funded by a fixed share of treasury and fee income, for use when outside credit is unavailable.

See the GOVC design white paper and Do Two Tokens Separate Financial Risk?, which stress-tests whether the separation holds.

Frequently asked questions

What is Side-Value Catcher tokenomics?

Side-Value Catcher tokenomics (사이드-밸류 캐처 토크노믹스), proposed by Jace Shim in Target Money (2026), is a dual-token design in which a payment token is kept as a stable, regulation-compliant settlement instrument, while a separate governance token captures value from the side of the system: platform fee revenue and interest on reserve assets. The two layers are kept apart so that governance-token volatility cannot impair payment stability.

Why separate a payment token from a governance token?

In the book’s argument, a single asset cannot be both a stable medium of exchange and an asset whose value grows with the ecosystem. Keeping the payment token stable and yield-free, and placing value capture in a separate governance token, prevents governance-token volatility from impairing payments.

How does the governance token capture value?

The book defines a quarterly cash flow, CF_total, as fee revenue across payment domains plus interest on reserve assets. That cash flow buys back and burns governance tokens, so the supply change equals −CF_total divided by the market price.

How is this different from Terra–Luna?

Terra’s stability depended on the market value of its sister token LUNA. In the Side-Value Catcher design, the governance token is not collateral for the payment token’s peg, and the payment token is backed one-to-one by reserve assets.

Does a buyback guarantee the governance token’s price?

No. The later GOVC white paper states that a buyback does not ensure price support and that expected future fees offer limited support for immediate withdrawals.
