What is metered machine-to-machine settlement?

Definition · Metered Machine-to-Machine SettlementMetered machine-to-machine settlement, as developed by Jace Shim (2026), is the movement of funds under a standing agreement based on measured service use, with a defined process for revising earlier measurements. Recording use, validating a reading, calculating the charge, and transferring funds can each have a different timestamp, and a correction is reconciled without replacing the original payment record.

Key ideaA signed reading can support a payment decision while still being revised later.

A five-minute reading and a daily payment

A data center pays a service provider for electricity delivered under a bilateral contract. One meter sends a reading every five minutes. At the end of the day, the meter operator discovers that part of the data was estimated while a device was offline, and the estimated record may already have prompted an automated transfer. The buyer and seller now have a pricing obligation that has changed and a transfer that has already happened.

Australia’s energy market operator describes changes to meter configurations for five-minute data; that example concerns an established market with its own rules. This model considers a separate bilateral payment workflow and holds the contractual pricing intervals fixed.

What the contract specifies

The contract specifiesWhy it matters

Treatment of estimated or missing dataDetermines how an estimated reading, such as one recorded while a device was offline, is handled
Who may change a readingSets the authority for revisions
How the counterparty can challenge the decisionGives the other side a route to dispute a revision
For compute contracts: an auditable unit, such as GPU-secondsAlso specifies how to handle failed or duplicated jobs

Charging a settlement window and booking corrections

Charge(W) = Σ agreed interval price × latest eligible quantity, for each interval in W

Correction = revised total − amount already allocated to W

Store each interval under a stable identifier and retain the sequence of authorized revisions. Record a correction as a debit or credit against the next bill or resolve it through the agreed refund procedure. A unique settlement reference links each transfer to the obligations it covers and helps prevent duplicate payouts. A five-minute price and a daily transfer can coexist when the daily bill sums each interval at its own price.

How much is earlier payment worth?

GExpected financing and nonpayment costs that faster collection avoids, after any burden shifted to the buyer
FAdditional transfer and conversion costs
RAdditional correction administration and expected unrecovered overpayments

Prefer fast settlement when G > F + R

Compare fast provisional payment with a slower validated batch at the same prices and consumption. Report components separately and track how often interim balances exceed the contractual exposure cap. At high usage, a capped unpaid balance can trigger an extra payment before the next scheduled batch.

Frequently asked questions

What is metered machine-to-machine settlement?

Metered machine-to-machine settlement, as developed by Jace Shim (2026), is the movement of funds under a standing agreement based on measured service use, with a defined process for revising earlier measurements. Recording use, validating a reading, calculating the charge, and transferring funds can each have a different timestamp, and a correction is reconciled without replacing the original payment record.

Should machines pay immediately on provisional meter readings?

According to the decision rule proposed by Jace Shim (2026), prefer fast settlement on the measured margin when G exceeds F plus R: G is the expected financing and nonpayment cost that faster collection avoids, F is additional transfer and conversion cost, and R is additional correction administration and expected unrecovered overpayments. Report the components separately.

How is a corrected meter reading reconciled after payment?

Store each interval under a stable identifier with its sequence of authorized revisions. A correction changes the obligation by the difference between the revised total and the amount already allocated to that settlement window, recorded as a debit or credit against the next bill or resolved through the agreed refund procedure, without replacing the original payment record.

Can five-minute prices be paid with a daily transfer?

Yes. A five-minute price and a daily transfer can coexist when the daily bill sums each interval at its own price. The charge for a settlement window is the sum of the agreed interval price times the latest eligible quantity for each interval in the window.

What does a correction look like in practice?

In the paper’s hypothetical interval, a provisional reading of 100 kWh at 0.12 currency units per kWh leads to a payment of 12. The approved revision is 90 kWh, so the seller owes a credit of 1.20; a validated batch would have included only 10.80 if the change arrived before its cutoff.

Does this apply to GPU and compute billing?

Yes, if the service defines an auditable unit such as GPU-seconds and specifies how to handle failed or duplicated jobs.

Does a confirmed stablecoin transfer discharge the obligation?

Not by itself. A stablecoin may carry an approved transfer, but technical confirmation of a transfer is one event in the workflow; the applicable contract and market rules determine whether the obligation is discharged. Compare stablecoins with an alternative rail under the same schedule, including fees, custody, and time to spendable proceeds.
