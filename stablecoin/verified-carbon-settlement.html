What is verified carbon settlement?

Definition · Verified Carbon SettlementVerified carbon settlement, as developed by Jace Shim (2026), is the release of an agreed payment against a specified mitigation record or registry event, with a procedure for later correction. Monitoring evidence supports a quantity estimate under a stated methodology, a registry records issued units and their status, and a payment record records the amount paid; each answers a different question.

The problemA funder can pay for a measured emissions reduction and later discover that the verified quantity was overstated.

The transfer has cleared. The reduction has changed.

A small industrial plant earns a performance payment for reducing methane emissions. The funder pays after a verifier signs off.

100 tCO₂e verified and paid for
→
85 tafter a calibration check

The transfer receipt still records the original amount. The relationship between the transfer and the revised environmental result depends on the contract and the correction process. The question is whether faster settlement can coexist with credible correction.

Three records, three questions

Monitoring evidenceHow much was reduced?Supports a quantity estimate under a stated methodology.
RegistryWhich units exist, and in what status?Where applicable, records issued units and their status.
Payment recordHow much was paid?Records the amount paid.

Outcome-finance contract

Can pay for independently verified mitigation without acquiring a credit or claiming an offset.

Credit purchase

Requires delivery of the agreed asset as defined by the relevant program and contract. Where retirement is included, retain the retirement reference and the party on whose behalf it was recorded.

Record the project identifier, monitoring period, methodology, evidence version, and applicable registry and unit identifiers. If funding the same project from multiple sources is permitted, the agreement should specify which amounts and claims may overlap.

Duplicate payment is not double counting

ProblemWhere it is checkedHow
Duplicate paymentPayment operations (an accounting problem)Give each payable installment an identifier linked to the obligation; a payment operator can flag a repeated request for the same installment before release
Double issuance, double use, double claimingThe relevant program or reporting levelAcross projects or registries, new identifiers can refer to overlapping activity; detecting it requires comparing the underlying projects, periods, and claim rights

A ledger can preserve the decision history and still depend on the quality and coverage of the records supplied to it.

A payment rule for revisions

qEligible quantity initially verified, in tonnes CO₂e
pContracted amount per tonne
hFraction withheld until a defined review point

Initial payment = p × q × (1 − h)

After the review, the payer recalculates total entitlement using the eligible corrected quantity. If corrected entitlement exceeds the amount already paid, the difference is released; if the amount paid exceeds entitlement, the excess becomes a recovery item under the agreement.

Handling different kinds of correction

Type of changeHow the contract can treat it
Clerical revisionsDistinguished from other changes in the contract
Changes in monitoring dataRecalculate entitlement on the eligible corrected quantity at the review point
Later changes to credit statusHandled under the agreement, including what happens to corrections identified after the review point
A contested or stale recordCan suspend the affected component pending independent review, while approved components remain payable where the contract permits

Corrections should be linked to the original record so that reviewers can reconstruct what each party knew when money moved.

When does stablecoin settlement help?

A fiat-referenced stablecoin can carry an approved payout where both parties can access the relevant payment and conversion services. Measure speed as the time funds become usable for the project, including conversion and withdrawal, and compare exchange spreads, custody, and reconciliation with the local alternative.

LimitThe same contract logic can operate on a conventional payment rail. A fast payout does not change the environmental methodology or recover an overpayment by itself.

Testing the design

ComparisonHold fixedTrack
Payment policyThe payment rail and verification methodImmediate release versus a defined holdback: time to usable proceeds, capital held back, downward revisions, unrecovered excess, and dispute costs; report smaller projects separately
Duplicate controlsThe same independent review processDuplicate flags and false positives across designs, distinguishing a repeated invoice from overlapping rights across registries
Payment railThe contract and verification processStablecoin versus conventional payouts; follow revisions after initial payout and report the observation cutoff and cases still open

The central proposition: holdbacks reduce unrecovered excess most where downward revisions are common and operators can bear the liquidity delay.

The framework supplies hypotheses and no empirical estimates.

Context and related papers

The Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market identifies tracking, independent verification, and no double counting among its Core Carbon Principles, and its assessment framework describes registry procedures for erroneous issuance and retirement records. The paper takes those functions as institutional inputs and asks how to design payment around them.

Frequently asked questions

What is verified carbon settlement?

What happens if a carbon reduction is revised after payment?

The payer recalculates total entitlement using the eligible corrected quantity after a defined review point. If corrected entitlement exceeds the amount already paid, the difference is released; if the amount paid exceeds entitlement, the excess becomes a recovery item under the agreement. The agreement must specify who can authorize revisions and what happens to corrections identified after the review point.

How does a holdback protect a carbon funder?

With quantity q, price p per tonne, and holdback fraction h, the initial payment is p × q × (1 − h). In the paper’s hypothetical example, 100 tonnes at USD 20 with a 10% holdback pays USD 1,800 and keeps USD 200 pending. If the accepted quantity falls to 85 tonnes, entitlement falls to USD 1,700, the pending amount is cancelled, and USD 100 remains to recover, compared with USD 300 under full upfront settlement.

What is the difference between duplicate payment and double counting?

A duplicate payment is an accounting problem that a payment operator can flag before release using installment identifiers. Double issuance, double use, and double claiming require different checks at the relevant program or reporting level, comparing underlying projects, periods, and claim rights across registries.

Is paying for verified mitigation the same as buying a carbon credit?

No. An outcome-finance contract can pay for independently verified mitigation without acquiring a credit or claiming an offset. A credit purchase requires delivery of the agreed asset as defined by the relevant program and contract, and where retirement is included, the retirement reference and the party on whose behalf it was recorded should be retained.
