What is the Care Governance Token (GOVC)?

Definition · Care Governance Token (GOVC)The Care Governance Token (GOVC) is a proposed governance instrument for the Care Cash (KRWC) architecture, designed by Jace Shim (2026) and described in Chapter 16 of Target Money. KRWC is intended for settlement; GOVC would participate in decisions within a defined mandate. The white paper summarizes the proposed allocation, governance, and financial dependencies for evaluation.

Key principleA token allocation and cash available in a crisis are different quantities.

Within ordinary votes

Fee allocation and capital discipline

The book considers using part of platform fee income for token-related incentives or buybacks and burns. Measure realized fee revenue, operating needs, and contractual reserve obligations before identifying any surplus. Disclose who can approve discretionary actions and how conflicts are managed, and assess liquidity and accounting effects independently of token price assumptions.

LimitsA buyback does not ensure price support, and expected future fees offer limited support for immediate withdrawals.

Backstops under joint stress

First identify liquid payment reserves, drawable external facilities, and separately funded emergency resources. Assign each source a legal owner, permitted use, availability deadline, and priority.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Care Governance Token (GOVC)?

The Care Governance Token (GOVC) is a proposed governance instrument for the Care Cash (KRWC) architecture, designed by Jace Shim (2026) and described in Chapter 16 of Target Money. KRWC is intended for settlement; GOVC would participate in decisions within a defined mandate. The white paper summarizes the proposed allocation, governance, and financial dependencies for evaluation.

Is GOVC a token offering?

No. The white paper documents a design proposal for evaluation and supplies neither an offering nor evidence of deployment, licensing, or regulatory classification. Operational parameters require validation and jurisdiction-specific review.

What can GOVC holders vote on?

Governance should specify which parameters can be changed by voting within a defined mandate, and place reserve eligibility, redemption rights, and access to sensitive user records outside ordinary discretionary votes.

Does a token buyback support the payment system?

Not necessarily. A buyback does not ensure price support, and expected future fees offer limited support for immediate withdrawals. Realized fee revenue, operating needs, and reserve obligations should be measured before identifying any surplus.

How should GOVC backstops be stress tested?

Stress governance-token value and liquidity at the same time as fees fall and redemption demands rise, exclude native token sales from critical redemption capacity in that joint-stress case, and test facility access and repayment.
