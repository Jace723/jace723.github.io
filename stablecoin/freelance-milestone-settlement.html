What is milestone-based settlement?

Definition · Milestone-Based Settlement ModelMilestone-based settlement, as modelled by Jace Shim (2026) in the working paper When Should Freelancers Get Paid?, assigns a price and an acceptance rule to each independently reviewable deliverable. The client funds a milestone before work starts, and when it is accepted, the escrow releases that installment through the agreed payment rail. The model makes explicit who carries unpaid accepted work and who carries locked funds at each stage of a project.

The trade-offPaying for accepted milestones reduces the balance left unpaid at the end of a project, while funding only the upcoming milestone can limit how much the client has tied up at any one time. That arrangement leaves subsequent milestones exposed to a client’s failure to fund them.

SettingA bilateral cross-border contract between a client and a freelancer

A project can be half delivered and fully unpaid

A developer is hired to build a reporting tool for a client abroad.

Milestone 1Data export accepted

The client accepts it and starts using it.

Milestone 2Dashboard delayed

The two sides disagree about a design change.

PaymentBoth sides wait

Under one final invoice, the dispute can delay payment for the export too. If the client prepaid the whole fee into escrow, its funds also stay locked while the dispute continues.

Milestone escrow already exists: Upwork describes fixed-price contracts in which clients fund milestones and may accept work, request changes, or let a review period expire. The contribution here is a way to compare funding depth, approval timing, and payout routes.

What the contract must specify

The contract specifiesRule of thumb from the paper
What counts as submission and who can accept itPrice and acceptance rule are set for each independently reviewable deliverable
When review is due and how silence is handledA missed deadline can trigger review or a release procedure agreed in advance; it should not silently convert a documented dispute into acceptance
How change requests are handledA change in scope calls for an amended price or milestone rule
How contested acceptance is resolvedThe parties still need a way to resolve ambiguous requirements and disagreements, even with automated test reports
What happens before an unfunded stageThe worker can pause before starting an unfunded stage; escrow guarantees funding only to the extent the funds are actually available under its terms

Where one deliverable depends on another, the parties should identify what can be reviewed or used in isolation. Slicing an indivisible deliverable into many invoices can simply multiply disputes.

Measuring who finances the project

UValue of accepted work whose proceeds are not yet usable to the worker
BClient funds held in escrow and unavailable for other uses

Unpaid-work exposure = Σ daily U · Escrow exposure = Σ daily B (currency-days)

Use one invoice currency and observe the project daily. Record completed work awaiting approval separately so a prolonged review does not disappear from the analysis.

Comparing designs

Convert exposures into financing costs using rates appropriate to each party. Add expected losses from nonpayment or mistaken release, review and dispute costs, and total payment and conversion charges, and measure the frequency and cost of interruptions when the next installment is not funded.

More milestones

Can reduce exposure, while adding reviews and fixed payout charges.

What decides it

The value of intermediate work and the ability to verify it reliably.

Where a stablecoin can make a difference

A stablecoin can carry an installment after release if both parties have reliable access to it and the recipient can obtain usable funds at acceptable cost. Measure the interval from approval to usable proceeds, including onboarding, custody, conversion, and withdrawal, and record the net domestic-currency amount after fees and exchange spreads.

CautionA fast transfer that remains in a wallet awaiting conversion does little for a worker who must pay expenses from a local bank account.

The design can also use fiat escrow and conventional bank transfers. Machine-assisted review of software can help check a documented acceptance criterion, but the parties still need a way to resolve ambiguous requirements and disagreements. Keep proprietary work and personal information in access-controlled systems and link the release to an invoice reference.

Include cancelled projects and workers who decline the payment method, and prespecify treatment of abandoned contracts, rejected milestones, and exchange-rate changes. The model predicts that payout-rail gains will be most visible where approval is already prompt and conversion is reliable.

The paper presents no causal estimates.

When should freelancers get paid?

The model compares designs by who carries the cost of waiting. Paying for each accepted milestone reduces the value of accepted work left unpaid, while funding only the upcoming milestone limits how much the client has locked up. The right design depends on the value of intermediate work and the ability to verify it reliably.

How do you measure who finances a freelance project?

Observe the project daily in one invoice currency. U is the value of accepted work whose proceeds are not yet usable to the worker, and B is the client’s balance held in escrow. Summing daily U and B gives unpaid-work exposure and escrow exposure in currency-days. Work completed but awaiting approval is recorded separately.

Is full-project escrow better than paying per milestone?

Not necessarily. In the paper’s hypothetical USD 3,000 contract with three milestones accepted on days 10, 20, and 30, full-project escrow funded on day zero and released on day 30 ties up USD 90,000-days of client funds, while sequential funding of each milestone ties up USD 30,000-days. An unfunded final invoice ties up no escrow but leaves the worker exposed to collection risk.

Should a missed review deadline count as acceptance?

A missed deadline can trigger review or a release procedure agreed in advance, but it should not silently convert a documented dispute into acceptance.
