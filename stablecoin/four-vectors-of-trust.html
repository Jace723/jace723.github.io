What is the Four Vectors of Trust Architecture?

Definition · Four Vectors of Trust ArchitectureThe Four Vectors of Trust Architecture (4대 신뢰 벡터 모델), proposed by Jace Shim in Target Money (2026), is a framework of four models that bind payment to verified evidence in markets where trust is costly: Agent–Objective Binding (Vector 1), Milestone-Based On-chain Settlement (Vector 2), On-chain Reputation and Dynamic Verification (Vector 3), and Exogenous Value Monetization (Vector 4). Each vector addresses a different source of the tax of distrust, and together they are meant to accumulate trust capital.

Core questionWhere does payment lose its purpose, and what evidence must travel with it so that trust comes from structure rather than from the goodwill of the parties?

What the vectors share

All four vectors rest on what the book calls the three layered properties of programmable money: a circulation layer that carries stable value, a contract layer that specifies the purpose, and a data and AI layer that verifies behavior. Each vector applies the same structure to a different market.

ValueCirculation layer: a stable payment token settles the amount
PurposeContract layer: the agreement fixes the condition for release
EvidenceData and AI layer: verified evidence decides whether the condition was met

The book pairs the vectors with a two-token structure, separating a payment token from a governance token, and with defenses against oracle failure and black-swan events.

See the three-layer model, Side-Value Catcher tokenomics, and scoped payment holds.

From the tax of distrust to trust capital

The book opens with the tax of distrust: the verification, audit, delay, and dispute costs that arise when money moves faster than the evidence of what it paid for. The four vectors are proposed as a way to reduce that tax at its source.

Trust capital is the book’s term for what accumulates when the risk premium for non-performance and diversion is reduced by design. In the book’s argument, suppliers recover working capital sooner, disputes and after-the-fact audits shrink, and competition shifts toward who sets the standard for binding payment to evidence.

What are the four vectors of trust?

Vector 1 is the Agent–Objective Binding Model for markets where the payer and beneficiary differ; Vector 2 is Milestone-Based On-chain Settlement for service and supply contracts; Vector 3 is On-chain Reputation and Dynamic Verification, which turns performance records into credit; and Vector 4 is the Exogenous Value Monetization Model for environmental and social value.
