Do two tokens actually separate risk?

Definition · Dual-Token Risk Separation TestThe dual-token risk separation test, proposed by Jace Shim (2026), measures risk separation between a payment token and a governance token by the payment system’s ability to serve redemptions and continue critical operations after the governance token and its supporting market are impaired.

Why functional separation is insufficientA payment token and a governance token can have different purposes while sharing the same sources of funding or operational control.

Proposed byJace Shim (심준규)
SourceZenodo working paper, Version 1.0 (2026), DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24612007 · PDF

TestRedemption and operating capacity at two stress horizons

What to map for each token

Payment instrument

Designed to settle claims at a stable denomination.

Governance instrument

May allocate voting power or participate in an economic arrangement whose value varies.

Map the legal claims, collateral, authorized operators, common counterparties, and committed funding for each.

Hidden channelsShared custody, fee-dependent reserve support, and emergency controls can carry distress across the boundary even where token contracts are distinct.

Stress test at two horizons

For each horizon: liquid resources available − due redemptions − protected operating outflows

RuleDetail
Asset availability and rail capacityCalculate both asset availability after haircuts and the capacity of the payout rail
Governance-token proceedsExclude contingent governance-token sale proceeds from a governance-failure scenario
External creditCount it only if the borrower can draw it under the modeled conditions, and show how it is repaid
Operational capacitySeparately test whether authorized payment operators retain the ability to process and reconcile redemptions

Applying the model

Report uncovered outflows, time to restore service, and which party bears the residual loss. Compare results before and after removing each linkage. Shared dependencies may be tolerable if they are explicit, bounded, and replaceable at stress horizons.

The proposed tests establish no safety or legal classification for any token.

Frequently asked questions

Do two tokens separate financial risk?

The dual-token risk separation test, proposed by Jace Shim (2026), measures risk separation between a payment token and a governance token by the payment system’s ability to serve redemptions and continue critical operations after the governance token and its supporting market are impaired.

Why isn’t having separate token contracts enough?

A payment token and a governance token can have different purposes while sharing the same sources of funding or operational control. Shared custody, fee-dependent reserve support, and emergency controls can carry distress across the boundary even where token contracts are distinct.

How does the stress test work?

For each horizon, subtract due redemptions and protected operating outflows from liquid resources available on that horizon, calculating asset availability after haircuts and payout-rail capacity. Governance-token sale proceeds are excluded in a governance-failure scenario, and external credit counts only if it can be drawn under the modeled conditions.

Can governance-token sales back stablecoin redemptions?

Not in a governance-failure scenario. In the paper’s example, next-day outflows of 30 against reachable reserves of 24 look covered with projected governance-token sales of 8, but removing those proceeds leaves a shortfall of 6.

When are shared dependencies acceptable?

Shared dependencies may be tolerable if they are explicit, bounded, and replaceable at stress horizons. The tests establish no safety or legal classification for any token.

Who proposed the dual-token risk separation test?

Jace Shim (심준규) proposed it in the working paper Do Two Tokens Separate Financial Risk? Stress testing payment and governance dependencies (Version 1.0, Zenodo, 2026, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24612007), developing the dual-token proposals in Chapters 7, 14, and 16 of his book Target Money.
