Who Controls Stablecoin Payment Networks?

A model of distribution dependence and bargaining power

What does the model explain?

DefinitionWho Controls Stablecoin Payment Networks? is a conceptual working paper by Jace Shim (2026) that develops a model of bargaining between fiat-backed stablecoin issuers and the platforms through which customers acquire, hold, spend, and redeem tokens. Issuance gives an issuer control over reserve management and token supply; distribution can give a wallet, exchange, or payment processor control over access to customers. The model predicts that a distributor’s share of joint surplus depends on how much payment activity would remain with the issuer if the relationship ended, how effectively the distributor could support a rival token, and how the parties bargain over the remaining gains from cooperation.

Research question

When do the interfaces through which customers acquire, hold, spend, and redeem stablecoins confer bargaining power on the platforms that operate them? A token may operate on multiple public chains while most customers in a particular market still use a small number of providers for onboarding, conversion, or merchant settlement.

Three drivers of a distributor’s share

1

Issuer’s outside option

How much payment activity would remain with the issuer if the relationship ended.

I₀

A better issuer outside option lowers distributor profit by β per unit.

2

Distributor’s outside option

How effectively the distributor could support a rival token.

D₀

A better distributor outside option raises distributor profit by 1 − β per unit.

3

Bargaining weight

How the parties bargain over the remaining gains from cooperation.

β

β lies between zero and one and sets each party’s share of S − I₀ − D₀.

Why dependence is corridor-specific

A distributor’s services may include identity onboarding, custody, fiat conversion, order routing, or merchant integration. Technical transferability and effective commercial switching can differ.

Easier to switch

  • The token listed in an app

Harder to switch

  • Merchant settlement
  • Local bank integrations
  • User balances held with a provider

The bargaining model

STransferable joint surplus from cooperation, before transfers and net of operating costs
I₀Issuer’s expected profit without an agreement
D₀Distributor’s expected profit without an agreement
βDistributor’s bargaining weight, between zero and one

Assume S exceeds I₀ + D₀ and that a contract can transfer value. A weighted Nash split implies:

Distributor profit = D₀ + β × (S − I₀ − D₀)

Issuer profit = I₀ + (1 − β) × (S − I₀ − D₀)

Holding S and β fixed, an improved issuer outside option reduces distributor profit by β per unit, and an improved distributor outside option raises distributor profit by 1 − β per unit. The model concerns profit under an agreement; the contract’s actual cash payment also reflects each party’s operating contribution and revenues earned directly.

Three testable propositions

P1

Issuer portability

Among otherwise similar agreements, access to independent distributors able to preserve merchant and fiat conversion coverage should improve issuer terms at renewal. Adding chains without adding usable alternatives should have a weaker association with those terms.

P2

Distributor substitutability

A distributor that can redirect customers to a credible rival stablecoin while preserving payment functionality should obtain better terms, holding its service contribution and issuer alternatives comparable. Credibility depends on supported corridors and redemption capacity.

P3

Concentration and bargaining

Distributor concentration should predict stronger bargaining outcomes chiefly where merchants cannot reroute settlement or users cannot easily switch fiat access providers. Balance concentration alone may reflect idle holdings and should be compared with corridor-level activity.

Observable case

Illustration, not identification

Circle and Coinbase

The paper cites Circle’s 2025 annual report, which describes arrangements under which payments to Coinbase depend in part on USDC held on its platform and on broader ecosystem allocations. The filing establishes that distribution and reserve-related economics can be contractually linked. A single disclosed arrangement cannot identify bargaining weights or establish the mechanism across the sector.

Where available, use negotiated distribution expense net of separately identifiable operating services, and distinguish payment flows from transfers between affiliated accounts. Strategic timing of integrations, contract confidentiality, and differences in costs limit causal interpretation; where payments are undisclosed, report observable terms and qualitative evidence rather than imputing contractual profit shares.

Relationship to existing research

Two-sided markets

Rochet and Tirole (2003) explain how platforms build usage by attracting both sides and allocating prices across them. This model focuses on the division of gains between an issuer and an access provider.

Companion model

The Stablecoin Reserve–Distribution Feedback Model studies how retained reserve income may finance payment network expansion. This paper asks how much of the cooperative surplus the issuer can retain to do so.

Implications

Two stablecoins with similar issuance can offer issuers different capacity to fund expansion if one relies on a distributor that is difficult to replace. For merchants and oversight authorities, a practical question is which provider can interrupt or redirect commercial access in a given corridor. The model gives a way to test how interoperability and operational alternatives shape that power.

The model is conceptual: the paper provides no statistical estimates, and its numerical example is hypothetical.

Frequently asked questions

Who controls stablecoin payment networks?

According to the model proposed by Jace Shim (2026), control is split. Issuance gives an issuer control over reserve management and token supply, while distribution can give a wallet, exchange, or payment processor control over access to customers. Which side captures more of the joint surplus depends on each side’s outside option and bargaining weight in a particular payment corridor.

Why can a wallet, exchange, or payment processor gain bargaining power over a stablecoin issuer?

Distributors may provide identity onboarding, custody, fiat conversion, order routing, or merchant integration. Where most customers in a market rely on a small number of such providers, little payment activity would remain with the issuer if the relationship ended, which weakens the issuer’s outside option and raises the distributor’s share of the surplus.

Does deploying a stablecoin on more blockchains reduce dependence on distributors?

Not necessarily. The paper distinguishes technical transferability from effective commercial switching. Proposition 1 predicts that adding chains without adding usable alternatives, such as independent distributors that preserve merchant and fiat conversion coverage, should have a weaker association with issuer terms at renewal.

What is the Circle and Coinbase example in the paper?

The paper cites Circle’s 2025 annual report, which describes arrangements under which payments to Coinbase depend in part on USDC held on its platform and on broader ecosystem allocations. The filing shows that distribution and reserve-related economics can be contractually linked; as a single disclosed arrangement, it cannot identify bargaining weights or establish the mechanism across the sector.

How does this model relate to two-sided market theory?

Two-sided market theory (Rochet and Tirole, 2003) explains how platforms build usage by attracting both sides and allocating prices across them. This model focuses instead on the division of gains between an issuer and an access provider.

How does it relate to the Stablecoin Reserve–Distribution Feedback Model?

The Reserve–Distribution Feedback Model (Shim, 2026b) studies how retained reserve income may finance payment network expansion. This paper examines how much of the cooperative surplus the issuer can retain and how dependence on a distributor affects that division. Two stablecoins with similar issuance can therefore offer issuers different capacity to fund expansion.
